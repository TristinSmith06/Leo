# Freed-Hardeman University (FHU) Overview
Freed–Hardeman University is a private university associated with the Churches of Christ and located in Henderson, Tennessee, United States. FHU is primarily undergraduate and residential. FHU also serves some commuting, part-time and adult students on-campus and through distance-learning programs.
FHU is governed by a board of trustees, all of whom are required to be members of Churches of Christ. FHU courses are offered by 12 academic departments in 5 colleges: Arts and Sciences, Biblical Studies, Business, Education and Behavioral Sciences, and the Honors College.

# History of Freed-Hardeman University
Freed–Hardeman traces its origin to the 1869 charter of a private high school and college for Henderson, the "Henderson Male Institute". FHU was known at various times as the "Henderson Masonic Male and Female Institute", "West Tennessee Christian College", or "Georgie Robertson Christian College". FHU was named Georgie Robertson Christian College after George Ann "Georgie" Robertson.

In the 1907 spring term Georgie Robertson Christian College closed. N. B. Hardeman, a Georgie Robertson Christian College alumnus, and A. G. Freed worked together to establish a new institution, the "National Teachers' Normal and Business College" to fill the educational void created in Henderson by the closing. The NTNBC was incorporated on May 21, 1907, but classes did not start until the fall of 1908. In 1919, it was renamed "Freed–Hardeman College" in honor of its founders. In February 1990, it became Freed–Hardeman University.

FHU did not accept African-American students until 1964. The first black graduate of FHU was Elizabeth Saunders in 1967.

FHU offers courses outside of the Henderson area in Memphis, Tennessee. FHU also offers European study abroad programs based at the FHU facility in Verviers, Belgium. Other study abroad opportunities at FHU include programs in Spain, Israel, Costa Rica, New York City, Canada, and Seoul.

FHU was granted an exception to Title IX in 2016 allowing discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation and gender identity where conflict exists with religious tenets.

During the 2019–2020 academic year, FHU university celebrated its 150th anniversary and a volume of university history, By the Grace of God: The Story of Freed–Hardeman University, was published.
