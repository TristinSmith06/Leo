David begins Psalm 27 with a question rooted in confidence: “The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?” (v. 1). David knew what it meant to face enemies, uncertainty, and danger. Yet he understood that the presence of trouble did not mean the absence of God. We often want God to remove everything that frightens us, but this psalm invites us to trust Him even while those things remain. Our courage grows as we remember who stands with us.

At the heart of the psalm, David reveals his deepest desire: to dwell in the Lord’s house and behold His beauty (v. 4). With so many urgent concerns surrounding him, David makes fellowship with God his greatest priority. That challenges us to examine what we seek most. We can become so consumed with asking God to change our circumstances that we neglect the invitation to draw closer to Him. Time spent in prayer, Scripture, and worship helps us see our circumstances through the assurance of His presence.

The psalm closes with an instruction many of us find difficult: “Wait on the LORD: be of good courage” (v. 14). Waiting requires faith because we cannot always see what God is doing or when an answer will come. Still, we can remain faithful in the responsibilities before us, pray honestly about our fears, and trust His goodness through the uncertainty. Today, whatever weighs on your heart, bring it to the Lord. Seek His face, take the next faithful step, and let Him strengthen your heart as you wait.
